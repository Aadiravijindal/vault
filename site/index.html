    The problem

    Your company's memory is locked inside twelve different tools.

    Each agent keeps its own notepad. The calling agent knows what the customer said. The chatbot doesn't. Claude Code knows why you dropped Redis. Emergent has never heard of it. Nobody can see all of it, and nobody can prove what any of it is based on.

  • The bad day

    Security

    One shared memory only works if you can trust what's in it.

    Ten checks run on every write, from every agent. Pass, hold and block are all logged the same way — the blocked one is what the auditor asks about.

    the gate · live
    support-copilotemail · billing@acme-invoicing.co

      01 / 10 — Identity

      Who is writing?

      Registered agent, valid credential, expected origin, allowed to write to this folder. An agent nobody registered gets blocked and raises an alert.

      02 / 10 — Channel trust

      Where did it come from?

      Fourteen tiers, set by architecture rather than by a filter's opinion. A logged-in employee is trusted. Email isn't — including internal email.

      03 / 10 — Source

      Is the sender real?

      SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Webhook signatures. mTLS. Lookalike and homoglyph domains caught before anything downstream reads them.

      04 / 10 — Private info

      45 detectors.

      Cards, IBAN, SSN, Aadhaar, medical records, cloud keys, private keys. Credentials are blocked outright — never masked and stored.

      05 / 10 — Sensitivity

      When unsure, label higher.

      Public, internal, confidential, secret, or your own labels. Uncertainty goes up and out to a person. It never guesses downward.

      06 / 10 — Walls

      Sales can't read HR. Ever.

      Hard isolation between departments and between projects, on read and on write. No admin bypass — break-glass takes two named humans and a time box.

      07 / 10 — Instructions

      Facts in. Commands out.

      A fact describes the world; an instruction tries to change what your agent does. Seven layers run together, and any one firing holds the write.

      08 / 10 — Policy rules

      Backtest before you enable.

      Write the rule in plain English. See exactly what it would have blocked over the last 90 days and what it costs your reviewers, before it's on.

      09 / 10 — Reconciliation

      Authority beats recency.

      When two facts clash, the one with standing wins — not the one that arrived last. The loser moves to history. It's never deleted.

      10 / 10 — Consent

      Are you allowed to keep it?

      Identifiable person, recorded lawful basis, still valid, not under erasure. No basis means hold and route to Privacy. It doesn't guess.

      The boring questions

      Answered up front.

      Deployment
      Nine models — cloud, your bucket, your VPC, on-premise, air-gapped, hybrid, hash-only where content never leaves you, and in-country sovereign.
      Keys
      BYOK through AWS, Azure or GCP KMS, or HYOK where keys never leave your HSM. Destroy the key and the data is gone, backups included.
      Records
      Verbatim archive, WORM, eDiscovery with Bates numbering, privilege logs. Mapped to SEC 17a-4, FINRA 4511 and 3110, MiFID II 16(7), FCA SYSC 10A.
      Employees
      One switch, with presets for the UK, Germany, India and more. No screen shows one named person's AI activity. No sentiment or productivity scoring — those code paths don't exist.
      Identity
      SAML 2.0, OIDC, SCIM 2.0 where deactivation kills the live session immediately, WebAuthn passkeys, geo-fencing that fails closed.
      Dates we track
      EU AI Act Article 50 from 2 Aug 2026; high-risk moved to 2 Dec 2027. India's DPDP Consent Manager 13 Nov 2026, full compliance 13 May 2027.
      Leaving
      Continuous mirror export to storage you own, an open schema, a self-host build, source escrow. Free and unthrottled. If we vanished overnight your memory is intact by morning.
      Pricing
      Per connected agent, governed fact volume and storage. Not per seat — headcount isn't what grows in an agent estate.
