The write path

Your agents read from memory. Almost nothing checks what got written to it.

An agent takes in an email, a support ticket, a web page, another agent's output — and writes what it learned somewhere it will read again tomorrow. That write usually happens with no identity check on the agent, no judgment about where the content came from, and no record that it happened at all.

It is a documented attack. Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 showed a hidden instruction in ordinary web content writing itself into an AI agent's long-term memory, where it persisted into every later session. OWASP now lists Memory and Context Poisoning as ASI06 in its 2026 Agentic AI Top 10.

The quieter problem is worse. A guess an AI made on Tuesday gets read back on Friday as though it were company policy, and nobody can tell the difference.

The gate

Ten checks. Every write. Every agent. No off switch.

The gate runs in every configuration Vault ships. It is not a setting, a plan tier or a toggle. Pass, hold, mask, block, escalate and quarantine are all logged the same way — because the blocked write is the one the auditor asks about.

Check 01 / 10 — Identity

Is this agent allowed to write here?

Registered, with named business and technical owners. Credential valid, used from an expected origin. Cleared for this folder, at this sensitivity, in this region. Model version still approved. Inside its rate budget. An unregistered agent writing to memory is blocked, and somebody is told.

Check 02 / 10 — Channel trust

Trust comes from where it came from, not from a score.

Fourteen tiers, decided by architecture rather than by a filter's opinion. A logged-in employee with MFA is trusted. An internal system of record is trusted. Email is not — including internal email. Neither are web forms, customer chat, external issue comments, scraped documents, third-party API responses, or another agent's output. The default is deny by source.

Check 03 / 10 — Source verification

Prove the sender is who the sender says.

SPF, DKIM and DMARC on mail. Signature verification on webhooks. mTLS between services. First contact from a new sender escalates. Domain age, sender reputation, threat feeds, and lookalike and homoglyph domains caught before anything downstream reads the content.

Check 04 / 10 — Private information

Forty-five detectors, and credentials never make it in.

Card numbers checked against Luhn, bank and IBAN and SWIFT, UPI IDs, SSN, Aadhaar, PAN, NINO, passports, national tax IDs, medical record numbers, ICD codes, cloud keys across nine providers, JWTs, private keys, connection strings, every GDPR Article 9 category, plus your own detectors. Mask, tokenise, redact, block or quarantine. Credentials are blocked outright — never masked and stored.

Check 05 / 10 — Sensitivity

When it isn't sure, it labels higher.

Public, internal, confidential, secret — or your own taxonomy. Uncertainty resolves upward and goes to a person. Vault never guesses a label downward.

Check 06 / 10 — Walls

What sales knows does not leak into what legal knows.

Hard isolation between departments, and between projects inside a department, enforced on read and on write. Cross-wall attempts are blocked, logged and alerted — never silently dropped. Walls apply to derived facts, summaries, search results and exports, so nothing leaks by inference either. There is no admin bypass: break-glass takes two named humans, a stated reason, a time box, and a loud entry in the log.

Check 07 / 10 — Instruction detection

A fact describes the world. An instruction tries to change what your agent does.

Only facts are stored. Seven layers run together — pattern, normalised text, structural position, statistical, classifier, semantic intent, and cross-reference against golden facts — and any one of them firing holds the write. It catches hidden text, invisible characters, metadata channels, encodings, homoglyphs, whitespace steganography, payloads split across messages, multilingual evasion, instructions in code comments, and text baked into images.

Check 08 / 10 — Policy rules

Write the rule in English. See what it would have done, then turn it on.

Nineteen rule types and eight actions, authored in plain language, compiled to policy-as-code, version controlled and exportable to git. Scope one globally or to a department, folder, project, entity or agent. Then backtest it against the last 90 days and get the exact list of what it would have held or blocked, an estimated false-positive rate, and how much reviewer time it adds — before it affects anything.

Check 09 / 10 — Reconciliation

Contradictions resolve by authority, not by whichever arrived last.

Golden beats everything. Then human-verified, then trusted channel, then higher organisational authority, then specific over general, then corroborated over single-source — and only then does newer beat older. Duplicates merge with their sources kept. The losing fact is never deleted, only moved to history. An attempt to overwrite a golden fact is blocked, alerted, and its source is flagged.

Check 10 / 10 — Consent and lawful basis

Is there a reason you're allowed to keep this about a person?

Is this about an identifiable person, is there a recorded lawful basis, is it still valid, is the purpose compatible, is this person under an active erasure request, is this a minor. No recorded basis means hold and route to Privacy. Vault does not guess this one.

If Vault can't reach its own checks, it queues the write and drains the queue later, or it refuses the write. There is no configuration in which a write passes through unchecked.

What survives

What's left is what the company actually knows.

Folders create themselves as facts arrive — by department, then by project or account — each with a named business owner and a named technical owner. A folder nobody owns is reported as a finding. The same customer named in a sales call, a support ticket and a bug report resolves to one entity.

Every fact carries its provenance when it is read: signed by a person, verified by a person, arrived over a trusted channel, or an unverified guess an AI made. So an agent can tell company policy from something another AI invented. So can a person.

The record

The record isn't ours to be trusted with.

Every write, hold, block, read, rule change, review decision, key rotation, legal hold, erasure and break-glass session lands in an append-only hash chain.

The chain makes tampering evident — but you hold the signing keys, so you can prove the record is intact without taking our word for it. Digests are anchored periodically to independent external witnesses. And a standalone verifier, licensed separately and importing nothing from our codebase, lets an auditor check the chain while trusting neither of us.

Golden facts

Some facts should only ever come from a person.

A golden fact is created by a named human with the authority to make it. No agent can write one. There is no API path that creates one.

Above a sensitivity threshold it takes two people. It is cryptographically signed and independently verifiable, dated for re-attestation, kept with its full change history, and ranked first every single time an agent reads — labelled, so the agent knows what it is holding. An agent that tries to overwrite one is blocked, and its source gets flagged.

With it on, no screen anywhere shows one named employee's AI activity. That is architecturally absent, not permission-gated. Law

Privacy law says delete it. Records law says keep it.

Vault does not pretend that conflict away. Material under a legal hold is locked but minimised to the least the hold actually requires, with a written record of which obligation applied and who decided — and it deletes itself the moment the hold lifts.

Erasure reaches the actual transcripts, not just the summaries: facts, derived facts, rolling summaries, read-log entries, backups and archive-tier objects. The methods are named honestly — hard delete, index purge, cache invalidation, crypto-shredding for backups, tombstone-plus-key-destruction for cold storage, read-log pseudonymisation, derived-fact regeneration — and you get a signed deletion receipt you can verify yourself.

Legal hold scopes by person, account, entity, project, folder, date range, matter or channel, and overrides retention expiry, hygiene decay and storage tiering. No administrator, including the account owner, can delete held material.

When it goes wrong

A dial, not a switch.

Warn, review-all, read-only, scoped freeze, read-block, full freeze — with a named administrator, in-flight writes queued rather than lost, and state that survives a restart.

Fourteen detectors watch for the attacks a single-write check cannot see: the same false claim arriving in fragments across sessions, a threshold value trending upward over weeks, coordinated sources, sleeper facts, volume anomalies, cross-wall probing, confidence laundering, and per-agent behavioural drift. Break-glass access takes two named approvers, a recorded reason, a time-boxed session, and notification to you — not just a line in a log.
