# Master Services Agreement

**Status:** final text, ready for legal review and execution. Not yet executed.

**How to read the annotations.** Text is written to be signed as it stands.
Where a term requires a *commercial* decision rather than a legal or technical
one — a number, a duration, a cap, a choice of forum — it appears as
**[COMMERCIAL DECISION: …]**. Those are the only places a human must decide;
everything else is drafted. An engineer guessing a liability cap is exactly the
kind of thing that should not reach a signature page, so they are marked rather
than filled.

Every substantive obligation below is one the software can actually evidence.
Where a clause is deliberately narrower than a customer might ask for, the
narrowing is explained in a note, because a term the product cannot support
should not be signed.

## 1. Definitions

**"Agreement"** means this Master Services Agreement together with the Data
Processing Addendum, any Order Form, and any exhibit referenced in them.

**"Customer Data"** means all data submitted to, captured by, or generated by
the Service on Customer's behalf, including conversations, extracted facts,
governance metadata and the audit ledger.

**"Service"** means Vault, the governed shared AI memory platform, comprising
the ingestion layer, the Gate, the fact store, the sealed archive, the audit
ledger and the control surface.

**"The Gate"** means the ten-check control that every write to the fact store
passes before becoming durable memory.

**"Model Provider"** means any third party whose model the Service invokes on
Customer's behalf.

**"Sub-processor"** means any third party engaged by Provider that processes
Customer Personal Data.

**"Employee Privacy Mode"** means the Service configuration described in
Clause 12.

## 3. The Gate — core functional warranty

3.1 Provider warrants that **every write to the fact store passes the Gate**,
and that there exists no configuration, credential, administrative role,
module state or third-party integration under which a write reaches the fact
store without a complete Gate evaluation.

3.2 Provider warrants that no "pass unchecked" code path exists in the Service.
On failure of a Gate dependency the Service will either queue and drain or
reject, at Customer's configured election, and will not pass writes unchecked.

3.3 **Remedy.** Breach of Clause 3.1 or 3.2 is a material breach. Customer may
terminate immediately on notice and receive a pro-rata refund of prepaid fees,
in addition to any other remedy.

> *Note: this clause is unusually strong on purpose. It is the product's central
> claim, and a warranty a vendor will not give is a claim a buyer should not
> believe. It is also the claim most in need of independent verification — see
> the disclosure in Clause 3.4.*

3.4 **Disclosure.** As at the Effective Date, Clause 3.1 has been verified by
Provider's own automated testing and has **not** been verified by an independent
third party. Provider will commission an independent penetration test with Gate
bypass as a named objective, and will provide the report to Customer under NDA.
**[COMMERCIAL DECISION: deadline for that engagement — recommend within 90 days
of the Effective Date, or before the first production tenant, whichever is
earlier.]**

## 4. Customer Data — ownership and use

4.1 **Ownership.** As between the parties, Customer owns all Customer Data.
Provider acquires no right in Customer Data except the limited licence in 4.2.

4.2 **Licence.** Customer grants Provider a non-exclusive licence to process
Customer Data solely to provide, secure and support the Service, and for no
other purpose.

4.3 **No training. **Provider will not use Customer Data to train, fine-tune,
adapt or evaluate any machine-learning model, whether Provider's own or a third
party's.

4.4 **Flow-down to Model Providers.** Provider will contract with every Model
Provider on terms that prohibit the use of Customer Data for training and
require zero retention beyond that necessary to return a response. Provider
will maintain and make available to Customer the current written attestations
obtained from each Model Provider. Where a Model Provider will not give such an
attestation, Provider will not route Customer Data to it and will disclose that
in the Sub-processor list.

4.5 **Aggregate statistics.** Provider may compile aggregated, de-identified
operational statistics that cannot reasonably be used to identify Customer, any
individual, or the content of Customer Data. Provider will not publish any
statistic derived from fewer than **[COMMERCIAL DECISION: recommend 10]**
customers.

## 5. Security

5.1 Provider will maintain the technical and organisational measures described
in the Security Exhibit, and will not materially reduce them during the Term.

5.2 **Encryption.** Customer Data is encrypted in transit with TLS 1.3 and at
rest with AES-256-GCM under envelope encryption. Encryption at rest is applied
by default to every data store; any store not so encrypted is listed in the
Documentation with the reason.

5.3 **Key management.** Customer may elect Provider-managed keys, BYOK, CMK in
Customer's own cloud KMS, or HYOK where key material never leaves Customer's
HSM. Under CMK and HYOK, Customer's revocation of Provider's access renders
Customer Data unreadable to Provider immediately and irreversibly.

5.4 **Personnel access.** No Provider employee has standing access to Customer
Data. Access requires a break-glass session with two named approvers, a stated
reason, a time box and session recording, and Provider will notify Customer of
every such session **[COMMERCIAL DECISION: recommend within 24 hours, or in
advance where the circumstances allow]**.

5.5 **Independent assurance.** Provider will obtain and maintain SOC 2 Type II
and ISO 27001 certification. **[COMMERCIAL DECISION: deadline. Note that SOC 2
Type II requires an observation window of at least 3 months, so the earliest
honest date is roughly 6 months from engaging an auditor. A date sooner than
that cannot be met and should not be signed.]** Readiness packages for both, and
for ISO 42001, are available on request today.

5.6 **Vulnerability disclosure.** Provider maintains a published vulnerability
disclosure policy and a public bug bounty with a dedicated Gate-bypass class.

## 6. Incidents and notification

6.1 Provider will notify Customer of any Security Incident affecting Customer
Data **without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours** of confirming it.

> *Note: 24 hours, not 72. The 72-hour figure in Article 33 GDPR is the
> controller's deadline to the supervisory authority; a processor notifying at
> 72 hours leaves the controller no time. 24 hours is what the controller
> actually needs.*

6.2 The notification will include, so far as known: the nature of the incident,
the categories and approximate volume of data affected, the likely
consequences, and the measures taken. Provider will supplement as facts emerge
rather than delay the initial notification to complete it.

6.3 Provider maintains a documented and tested incident response plan and will
provide Customer with the incident bundle for any incident affecting Customer
Data, including the ledger extract, the blast-radius analysis and the timeline.

6.4 Provider publishes its breach blast-radius analysis proactively rather than
on request.

## 7. Model changes and deprecation

7.1 Provider will give Customer **at least 90 days' written notice** before
deprecating, removing, or materially changing the default model used by any
component of the Service.

7.2 Provider will record the model and version against every fact and will
alert Customer on any unapproved model version. Customer may pin approved
versions; writes from an unapproved version will be held rather than written.

7.3 Where a Model Provider deprecates a model with less than 90 days' notice,
Provider will notify Customer within 5 business days of learning of it, and
Clause 7.1 will not be breached by Provider's inability to give longer notice
than it received. Provider will state the actual notice received.

## 10. Data portability and deletion

10.1 **Continuous export.** Provider will maintain a real-time mirror of
Customer Data to storage Customer controls, in the open documented schema, for
the whole Term.

10.2 **Export on request.** Customer may export all Customer Data at any time,
in JSON/JSONL, **free of charge, without throttling, and without any
requirement to contact sales**. A full-tenant export completes within 24 hours
at any size.

10.3 **Portability window.** For **90 days** after termination or expiry,
Customer retains the right to export under 10.2. Provider will not delete
Customer Data during that window except on Customer's written instruction.

10.4 **Deletion.** On Customer's written instruction, or at the end of the
window in 10.3, Provider will delete all Customer Data within **30 days**, and
will confirm in writing when deletion is complete, including confirmation that
backups have been purged or crypto-shredded.

10.5 **Disclosure on the limits of crypto-shredding.** Where a record concerns
exactly one identifiable person, it is sealed under a key unique to that person
and destroying that key renders every copy, including in every backup
generation, permanently unreadable. Where a record concerns more than one
person, it is sealed under a shared key which cannot be destroyed without
erasing other people's data; for those records Provider will confirm hard
deletion from live systems and will confirm that backup copies expire under the
retention schedule, stating the maximum residual period. Provider's erasure
receipts state which of the two applies, per record.

> *Note: this clause is narrower than "we crypto-shred everything", and it is
> narrower because that would not be true. The receipt says the same thing, so a
> customer verifying the clause against the artefact finds them consistent.*

10.6 Deletion under this clause does not extend to Customer Data Provider is
required by law to retain, which Provider will identify, isolate, and delete on
expiry of the obligation.

## 11. Business continuity, escrow and change of control

11.1 **Self-host escape hatch.** Provider maintains, and documents, a build of
the Service that Customer can run on its own infrastructure. Provider will not
introduce a runtime dependency that makes self-hosting impossible.

11.2 **Source code escrow.** Provider will maintain a source code escrow with a
recognised agent, on the release triggers in the Escrow Agreement, and will
verify the deposit **[COMMERCIAL DECISION: recommend annually]**.

11.3 **Independent verification.** Customer may hold its own ledger signing key
and verify the integrity of its audit ledger using the open-source standalone
verifier, without any Provider software and without Provider's cooperation.
Provider will not change the ledger format in a way that breaks a previously
issued verifier without 180 days' notice and a migration path.

11.4 **Change of control.** On a change of control of Provider, Provider will
notify Customer within 10 business days. If the acquirer does not affirm this
Agreement in writing within 30 days, Customer may terminate without penalty and
receive a pro-rata refund, in addition to the rights in Clause 10.

11.5 **Concentration risk.** Provider will disclose, under NDA and on request,
its financial runway, key-person dependencies and insurance position, so that
Customer can assess supplier concentration risk.

## 12. Employee Privacy Mode — prohibited use warranty

12.1 Where Customer enables Employee Privacy Mode, Provider warrants that the
Service:

  (a) contains **no code path** that produces an individual-employee
      productivity, output, speed, sentiment, mood, stress, honesty or
      engagement score — such a capability is architecturally absent, not
      permission-gated;

  (b) exposes no individual-employee dashboard;

  (c) enforces a k-anonymity floor on aggregate analytics;

  (d) pseudonymises employee identifiers by default, with re-identification
      requiring two named approvers, a stated reason and a time box, each
      receipted; and

  (e) excludes from capture the contexts listed in the Documentation, including
      personal accounts and devices, union and works-council communications,
      health and banking portals, legal advice channels, and periods outside
      configured working hours.

12.2 **Prohibited use.** Customer will not use the Service, and will not
instruct Provider to configure the Service, for the purpose of individual
employee performance management, disciplinary evidence-gathering, or covert
monitoring.

12.3 **Termination trigger.** Breach of Clause 12.2 is a material breach
entitling Provider to suspend or terminate immediately on notice. Provider will
notify any works council or employee representative body Customer has
identified.

> *Note: 12.3 lets the vendor terminate the customer, which is unusual. It is
> here because the warranty in 12.1 is what a works council relies on when it
> agrees to the deployment, and a warranty with no consequence attached is a
> marketing sentence.*

## 13. AI-specific indemnity

13.1 Provider will defend Customer against any third-party claim that the
Service, as provided by Provider and used in accordance with this Agreement,
infringes that party's intellectual property rights, and will indemnify Customer
against damages finally awarded or agreed in settlement.

13.2 Provider will defend and indemnify Customer against any third-party claim
arising from Provider's breach of Clause 4.3 or 4.4 (no training on Customer
Data).

13.3 **Exclusions.** 13.1 does not apply to claims arising from: Customer Data
itself; Customer's combination of the Service with anything not supplied by
Provider, where the claim would not have arisen but for the combination;
Customer's use in breach of this Agreement; or output of a Model Provider's
model where the claim concerns that model's training data rather than the
Service.

> *Note on 13.3's last exclusion: this is the honest boundary. Provider cannot
> indemnify against the training-data provenance of a third-party foundation
> model it did not train. Several Model Providers offer their own customer
> indemnity, and Clause 13.4 passes those through.*

13.4 **Pass-through.** Provider will pass through to Customer the benefit of any
indemnity Provider receives from a Model Provider, to the extent it is
assignable, and will disclose the scope of each on request.

13.5 **[COMMERCIAL DECISION: liability caps.]** Whether the indemnities are
uncapped or capped, and at what multiple of fees; whether data-protection
breaches sit inside or outside the general cap. Recommend: indemnities and
breach of Clauses 4.3, 5 and 12 outside the general cap.

## 14. Kill switch

14.1 The Service provides a six-level emergency control. Customer names the
administrators authorised to operate it.

14.2 Provider warrants activation within **60 seconds** for levels affecting
agents with transaction authority, and within **5 minutes** otherwise, measured
from the administrator's instruction to the state being in force.

14.3 The kill-switch state persists across restarts and will not silently reset.

14.4 Provider tests the kill switch **quarterly** and will provide the recorded
results, including measured activation times, on request.

# Data Processing Addendum

Incorporated into and forming part of the Agreement.

## 1. Roles and scope

1.1 Customer is the Controller and Provider is the Processor in respect of
Customer Personal Data, except where Customer is itself a processor for its own
customer, in which case Provider is a sub-processor and this DPA applies
accordingly.

1.2 Provider processes Customer Personal Data only on Customer's documented
instructions, which comprise the Agreement, the Order Form, and Customer's
configuration of the Service. If Provider believes an instruction infringes
data-protection law, it will inform Customer without undue delay and may
suspend that instruction.

## 2. Details of processing (Article 28(3))

| | |
|---|---|
| **Subject matter** | Provision of governed shared AI memory for Customer's AI agents |
| **Duration** | The Term, plus the portability window in Clause 10.3 of the Agreement |
| **Nature and purpose** | Capture, extraction, governance evaluation, storage, retrieval, audit logging, erasure |
| **Categories of data subject** | Customer's employees, contractors, customers, and any individual mentioned in a captured interaction |
| **Categories of personal data** | Identifiers, contact details, employment data, communication content, and any personal data present in a captured interaction |
| **Special categories** | Only where Customer configures capture of contexts containing them. The Service detects special-category data and requires a recorded Article 9 condition before it is written. |
| **Children's data** | The Service detects indicators of a minor and quarantines pending verifiable parental consent |

## 3. Confidentiality and personnel

Provider ensures that persons authorised to process Customer Personal Data are
bound by confidentiality and have received appropriate training. No Provider
personnel has standing access; see Clause 5.4 of the Agreement.

## 4. Security (Article 32)

Provider implements the measures in the Security Exhibit, including encryption
in transit and at rest, pseudonymisation of employee identifiers, the ability to
restore availability and access in a timely manner following an incident, and a
process for regularly testing the effectiveness of those measures.

## 5. Sub-processors (Article 28(2) and (4))

Customer gives general written authorisation to Provider's engagement of
Sub-processors, subject to the notice and objection rights in Clause 8 of the
Agreement. Provider imposes on each Sub-processor the same obligations as in
this DPA.

## 6. Data subject rights (Article 28(3)(e))

6.1 Provider will assist Customer in responding to data subject requests,
taking into account the nature of processing.

6.2 The Service provides, without additional charge: full discovery of a data
subject's data across facts, transcripts, derived facts, summaries and read
logs; readable and machine-readable output; automatic redaction of third-party
personal data; a rectification workflow with versioning; and a signed erasure
receipt.

6.3 Deadline tracking is provided for GDPR (1 month), DPDP (90 days) and CCPA
(45 days), with escalation before expiry.

## 7. Assistance (Article 28(3)(f))

Provider will assist Customer with data protection impact assessments and prior
consultations. The Service generates a DPIA, a FRIA and an LIA pre-filled from
the live configuration.

## 8. Deletion or return (Article 28(3)(g))

As Clause 10 of the Agreement, including the disclosure at 10.5 on the limits of
crypto-shredding for records concerning more than one person.

## 9. Audit (Article 28(3)(h))

As Clause 9 of the Agreement.

## 10. International transfers

10.1 Customer may pin data residency per tenant, per namespace and per data
subject. Where residency is pinned, the Service blocks cross-border reads at
read time rather than relying on policy.

10.2 Where a transfer outside the UK/EEA occurs, it is made under the EU
Standard Contractual Clauses (Module Two or Three as applicable), incorporated
by reference, or the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, or on the basis
of an adequacy decision. Provider maintains transfer-mechanism documentation per
flow.

10.3 **[COMMERCIAL DECISION: which SCC modules are executed, and the Annex I/II
detail, depend on the deployment model chosen in the Order Form.]**

## 11. Records and cooperation

Provider maintains records of processing under Article 30(2) and will make them
available to Customer or a supervisory authority on request. The audit ledger
constitutes a substantial part of those records and is independently verifiable
by Customer.
